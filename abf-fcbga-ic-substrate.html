Engineering & sourcing notes

工程与采购要点

Substrate yield is dominated by the mismatch between silicon, mould compound and organic build-up CTE. Every extra build-up layer adds one more interface where that mismatch shows up as warpage.

Process & technical notes

  • Build-up layers are laminated alternating ABF film and patterned copper; the number of lamination cycles fixes both the registration budget and the accumulated residual stress.
  • Fine line/space below a few micrometres pushes the patterning toward semi-additive routes, where seed-layer control rather than exposure resolution sets the yield.
  • Large-body packages need a core or stiffener strategy; changing the core material to control warpage also changes the drilling and plating behaviour of the through vias.

Buyer checklist

  • State the body size and the build-up layer structure explicitly; a layer count quoted without a body size is not a comparable offer.
  • Request warpage data at room and reflow temperature, since room-temperature flatness alone does not predict assembly yield.
  • Confirm the ABF film and core material availability for the intended volume before tooling, as public reporting describes long equipment and material lead times.

QC & inspection focus

  • Review open/short test coverage per net and cross-section the build-up interfaces for voids and registration.
  • Match the MSL classification and temperature-cycling report to the exact substrate revision in the BOM, not to a family-level qualification.

These notes are compiled per category from public engineering standards and the failure modes buyers most often meet. Figures are typical industry ranges; the binding version is the one confirmed against your drawing, target market and RFQ.

  • Warpage and lamination yield degrade together as body size grows; a substrate maker qualified at one size is not automatically qualified at a larger one on the same design rules.
  • The layer notation such as 18+n+18 describes build-up symmetry around a core; changing it changes residual stress balance even when the total layer count is similar.
  • CTE mismatch between silicon, mould compound and organic build-up is the dominant driver of package warpage, so a substrate-only flatness figure does not predict assembly yield.
  • Embedded passive or IPD options reduce the power delivery path but add a lamination step and a new void-inspection requirement that must be in the qualification plan.

A real trade-off at RFQ stage

A buyer increases an AI ASIC package body size to fit more HBM stacks and keeps the same substrate layer structure. The quoted lead time jumps because the larger body needs a different stiffener strategy and a new warpage qualification. We normally separate the RFQ into two decisions — the layer structure and the body-size qualification — and ask for room- and reflow-temperature warpage data on the exact revision before the package sign-off.

FAQ

常见问题

What is the minimum order quantity (MOQ)?

500 pcs per item. Mixed-SKU consolidation across one shipment is allowed.

How fast is the quote?

Standard items are quoted within 48 hours by an engineer, with lead time and Incoterms stated up front.

How can I verify the factory remotely?

By-process photo gallery, recorded shop-floor video, a certificate & document wall, and live inspection by appointment.

What are the payment terms?

T/T to Shenzhen Senya Industrial Co., Ltd.: 30% deposit to start, 70% before shipment (ship after full payment).

Do you handle finished or powered devices?

Yes. We integrate assembled electronics and PCBA, and consolidate finished devices into one shipment with a full export document package.

Why is body size such a strong constraint?

Warpage and lamination yield degrade as the substrate gets larger. A fabricator qualified at one body size is not automatically qualified at a larger one.

Are the reported ABF shortage figures your forecast?

No. They are publicly reported 2026 industry figures cited as buyer context. Availability and lead time are confirmed per lot at RFQ.

Do you certify substrates to JEDEC standards?

No. JEDEC references are public editorial references; MSL and temperature-cycling evidence comes from the substrate maker and the buyer's package qualification.

What do you need to quote a substrate?

A package drawing with body size, ball map and pitch, build-up layer structure, line/space minimum, target warpage limit and the required reliability test plan.

Is a glass-core substrate available now?

Public 2026 reporting describes it as an active development area with commercialisation hurdles. Treat it as a roadmap topic, not a standard production option.

Can you source BT substrates for non-AI packages?

Yes. BT-core substrates remain common for smaller packages; the structure is matched to the package drawing at RFQ.
