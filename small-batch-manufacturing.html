How the engagement works

合作如何推进

Small-batch and bridge production exists to de-risk a ramp: it validates the line, the fixture and the supply chain before a full-volume commitment.

What we do, step by step

  • A bridge run uses production tooling and the real line at low volume, so yield data is representative rather than hand-built.
  • Fixtures, work instructions and test coverage are proven on the bridge lot and then carried unchanged into volume.
  • Material is ordered to the pilot quantity with a documented scale-up path to the volume BOM.

What to prepare as a buyer

  • Use the bridge run to validate packaging and shipping, not only the product.
  • Freeze the design before the bridge lot; changes after this point invalidate the yield baseline.
  • Ask for a scale-up cost curve so the volume unit price is known before commitment.

How we verify & report

  • Full inspection on the bridge lot with a per-unit data sheet, rather than AQL sampling.
  • Line-changeover and first-piece/last-piece checks documented as the volume-run baseline.

Break-even tooling, traceability and pilot yield

硬模盈亏平衡、追溯与试产良率

These notes are compiled per category from public engineering standards and the failure modes buyers most often meet. Figures are typical industry ranges; the binding version is the one confirmed against your drawing, target market and RFQ.

  • Small-batch economics are dominated by set-up amortisation and material minimums: a resin sold in 25 kg bags or a fabric with a 300 m dye-lot minimum sets a floor that has nothing to do with the part count.
  • Rapid processes (CNC from billet, 3D printing, soft tooling, laser-cut sheet) trade unit cost for lead time; the break-even quantity should be stated so the buyer knows when to move to hard tooling.
  • Traceability matters more, not less, at low volume: with few units a single deviation is a large fraction of the lot, so each unit should carry a serial-linked inspection record.
  • A pilot lot and a production lot should not be quoted from the same yield assumption; first-article scrap, learning-curve time and engineering change orders belong in the pilot price.

A real trade-off at RFQ stage

A buyer wants 50 assembled units now and 5,000 later. Quoting both on one hard-tool BOM overcharges the 50 and under-specifies the 5,000; we normally define a bridge plan — soft-tooled pilot, then hard tooling with a documented changeover point.

FAQ

常见问题

What is the minimum order quantity (MOQ)?

500 pcs per item. Mixed-SKU consolidation across one shipment is allowed.

How fast is the quote?

Standard items are quoted within 48 hours by an engineer, with lead time and Incoterms stated up front.

How can I verify the factory remotely?

By-process photo gallery, recorded shop-floor video, a certificate & document wall, and live inspection by appointment.

What are the payment terms?

T/T to Shenzhen Senya Industrial Co., Ltd.: 30% deposit to start, 70% before shipment (ship after full payment).

Do you handle finished or powered devices?

Yes. We integrate assembled electronics and PCBA, and consolidate finished devices into one shipment with a full export document package.

What is the minimum batch you accept?

500 pcs per SKU; trial runs and mixed-SKU consolidation are supported.

Do you do pilot runs before mass production?

Yes. Pilot batches validate design and process before scaling.

Can I iterate quickly on a small batch?

Yes. Low-volume runs are built for fast design iteration.

Is small-batch production economical?

Consolidating multi-SKU orders keeps small batches cost-effective.

Can a bridge run ship to customers?

Yes, if the lot passes the same release criteria as production; we mark it as a pilot lot in the traceability record so any field feedback is easy to isolate.

How does small batch relate to the 500 pcs MOQ?

Engineering and pilot lots can be below 500 pcs; the 500 pcs per item MOQ applies once the programme moves to a repeatable production run.
