Engineering & sourcing notes

工程与采购要点

At 224G-class rates the loss budget is split between dielectric loss, conductor loss and glass-weave skew. Changing only the resin grade usually moves less of the budget than buyers expect.

Process & technical notes

  • Low-Dk cloth and Q-glass reduce dielectric loss but change the weave-induced skew pattern; the routing angle and spread-glass option must be reviewed with the fabricator.
  • HVLP foil lowers conductor loss by reducing surface roughness, but adhesion and peel strength then depend on the oxide treatment rather than on roughness alone.
  • Hydrocarbon and PTFE resin routes differ in press temperature and drilling behaviour; a mixed route needs separate lamination cycles, which is why hybrid stack-ups cost more.

Buyer checklist

  • Request Dk/Df at one stated test frequency and method, plus the foil roughness figure on the same datasheet revision.
  • Define the loss budget per channel and which layers need high-grade material, instead of specifying M9 across the whole stack-up.
  • Ask for the insertion-loss coupon design and the measurement report of the exact laminate lot, not a generic grade datasheet.

QC & inspection focus

  • Compare coupon S-parameters across the lot and against the simulated channel budget before releasing a production build.
  • Check resin-content uniformity and glass-cloth alignment on a cross-section, since local Dk variation shows up as impedance spread.

Loss budget split: dielectric, conductor and glass weave

插损预算拆分：介质、导体与玻纤织纹

These notes are compiled per category from public engineering standards and the failure modes buyers most often meet. Figures are typical industry ranges; the binding version is the one confirmed against your drawing, target market and RFQ.

  • At 224G-class rates the conductor loss from copper roughness can rival the dielectric loss, so upgrading the resin while keeping HTE foil often yields a smaller gain than the grade change suggests.
  • Low-Dk and Q-glass reinforcement reduce dielectric loss but change the weave-induced skew; a spread weave and a routing angle rule must be reviewed together with the fabricator.
  • Hydrocarbon and PTFE resin routes need different press temperatures, and a hybrid stack-up that mixes them requires separate lamination cycles, which is the main cost driver.
  • A grade name such as M8 is a supplier-side family label, not a cross-vendor standard; two M8 materials are not interchangeable without re-running the channel simulation.

A real trade-off at RFQ stage

A buyer specifies M9 across the whole 24-layer stack-up to protect a few 224G links. The material cost and lead time roughly double, while only four layers actually need that Df. We normally present a hybrid build — high-grade material on the critical layers, mid-grade elsewhere — with two loss-budget simulations, so the trade-off is decided before the press tooling is ordered.

FAQ

常见问题

What is the minimum order quantity (MOQ)?

How fast is the quote?

How can I verify the factory remotely?

What are the payment terms?

Do you handle finished or powered devices?

Yes. We integrate assembled electronics and PCBA, and consolidate finished devices into one shipment with a full export document package.

Why is Df not enough to compare two laminates?

Conductor loss from copper roughness and glass-weave skew also consume the budget. Ask for Dk/Df at one stated frequency plus foil Rz and the insertion-loss coupon result.

Is M9 always required for an AI server board?

Not necessarily. Many designs use a hybrid build with high-grade material only on the fastest layers, which is a cost and availability decision made at RFQ.

Do you certify the material to PCIe or IEEE specifications?

No. Those are public editorial references for the channel standard; conformance evidence comes from the fabricator and the buyer's own SI testing.

What is driving long lead times on high-grade laminate?

Public 2026 reporting points to low-Dk glass cloth, HVLP foil and lamination capacity as the constrained links. Lead times are indicative and confirmed per lot at RFQ.

Is Q-glass mandatory for 224G channels?

No. It is one option among spread low-Dk glass weaves. Necessity depends on the channel length and loss budget, not on the headline data rate.

Can you quote material only, without fabrication?

Yes. Material-only enquiries are consolidated by lot and can be shipped to a buyer's own fabricator with the COA and lot traceability attached.
