Engineering & sourcing notes

工程与采购要点

EV charging hardware is a standards and thermal problem: IEC 61851 / 62196, connector type and cable heat rise set the enclosure and BOM before styling.

Process & technical notes

  • AC vs DC topology is fixed by the standard and connector (Type 2, CCS1/CCS2, GB/T); a connector change re-opens safety testing.
  • Cable cross-section and terminal crimp are qualified by temperature-rise test at rated current, not by conductor gauge alone.
  • RCD/protection, contactor and metering are laid out to keep creepage and heat sources apart in a sealed IP54 enclosure.

Buyer checklist

  • Name the destination market and connector standard at RFQ; CE, UL and GB/T certification paths do not overlap.
  • Specify rated power, single/three-phase and cable length; these drive thermal design and the bill of materials.
  • Require an IP and IK rating plus a salt-spray plan for outdoor wall-box units.

QC & inspection focus

  • Hi-pot, insulation resistance and continuity test on every unit, plus RCD trip-time verification.
  • Full-load temperature-rise test on cable and terminals with logged thermocouple data per pilot lot.

Charging standards, RCD scheme and derating

充电标准、漏保方案与降额

These notes are compiled per category from public engineering standards and the failure modes buyers most often meet. Figures are typical industry ranges; the binding version is the one confirmed against your drawing, target market and RFQ.

  • AC wallbox and DC fast-charge components live under different standards: IEC 61851 for the charging system, IEC 62196 for connectors, and DIN 70121 / ISO 15118 for communication — a control board qualified for one is not automatically valid for the others.
  • Contactor, relay and cable ratings must be specified for continuous current at 40 °C ambient and for the prospective short-circuit current, not just a nominal 32 A / 63 A label.
  • Residual-current protection for EVSE has a specific requirement (Type A plus 6 mA DC detection or Type B per IEC 62955 / IEC 62423); substituting a general Type A RCD is a common and costly compliance gap.
  • Outdoor enclosures need a defined IK impact rating alongside IP, plus UV and salt-fog data for the cable and housing polymer; a UV-untested PC/ABS blend yellows and embrittles in field sunlight.

A real trade-off at RFQ stage

A buyer wants one wallbox platform for EU and North America. Connector (Type 2 vs J1772/NACS), grid metering and RCD rules diverge; we usually quote a shared power stage with region-specific connector, metering and approval packs, so the certification file stays valid in each market.
