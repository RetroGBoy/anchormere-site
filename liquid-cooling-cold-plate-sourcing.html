Engineering & sourcing notes

工程与采购要点

A cold plate is a pressure vessel as much as a heat exchanger. Thermal resistance, pressure drop and leak tightness are coupled: adding fins improves the first and worsens the second, while a thinner base improves both and raises the warpage risk.

Process & technical notes

  • Skived fins are formed from the base itself, so there is no braze interface to fail, but fin height and pitch are limited by the skiving tool and the base thickness.
  • Vacuum brazing allows closed internal channels and complex manifolds, but flux residue and void area at the braze line become the dominant leak and reliability risks.
  • Base flatness directly sets the bond-line thickness of the thermal interface material; a flatness spec without a clamping condition is not a usable requirement.

Buyer checklist

  • Provide heat load, coolant type and inlet temperature, allowable pressure drop at the design flow, and the mounting hole pattern with clamping torque.
  • Specify leak rate together with the test method, test pressure and hold time, and require a serial-numbered leak record per part.
  • Confirm coolant compatibility for every wetted material — base, braze alloy, seals and fittings — before the loop is filled.

QC & inspection focus

  • Run a helium or pressure-decay leak test on 100% of parts and record the flow-versus-pressure-drop curve per lot.
  • Measure base flatness in the clamped condition and inspect the braze line by cross-section or X-ray on the qualification lot.

Thermal resistance, pressure drop and leak-test boundaries

热阻、压降与泄漏测试边界

These notes are compiled per category from public engineering standards and the failure modes buyers most often meet. Figures are typical industry ranges; the binding version is the one confirmed against your drawing, target market and RFQ.

  • Adding fin density improves thermal resistance but raises pressure drop, which increases pump power; the two cannot be optimised independently of the loop design.
  • Skived fins have no braze interface to fail, but fin height and pitch are limited by the skiving tool; vacuum-brazed closed channels allow complex manifolds at the cost of a braze-void risk.
  • A leak rate specified without a test method, test pressure and hold time is not comparable between two suppliers, even when the numeric value looks identical.
  • Base flatness only has meaning under the clamping condition used in assembly; measuring a free plate can pass a part that lifts the die corner under torque.

A real trade-off at RFQ stage

A buyer specifies a very low thermal resistance and a tight pressure-drop cap on the same cold plate. Denser fins meet the first target but push the pump beyond its curve. We normally return two geometries with measured flow-versus-pressure-drop curves and the corresponding thermal figures, so the loop pump is selected against real data rather than a single heat-exchanger number.

FAQ

常见问题

What is the minimum order quantity (MOQ)?

What are the payment terms?

Do you handle finished or powered devices?

Yes. We integrate assembled electronics and PCBA, and consolidate finished devices into one shipment with a full export document package.

What decides whether a cold plate is machined or brazed?

Channel geometry, fin density and the required pressure drop. Fine skived fins and complex internal manifolds usually push the design toward a brazed or multi-piece build.

Do you certify parts to OCP or ASHRAE standards?

No. OCP and ASHRAE documents are public editorial references for interface and coolant-class context; dimensional and leak evidence comes from the part maker.

What leak rate should be specified?

It depends on the coolant and loop pressure. The leak rate, test method, test pressure and hold time are defined together at RFQ rather than as a single number.

Can thermal parts ship with the PCBA order?

Yes. Cold plates, fittings and manifolds are consolidated into the same multi-SKU shipment with the board assembly and the export document package.

Is a copper plate always better than aluminium?

Copper conducts better, but aluminium is lighter and cheaper and can be adequate with the right channel design. The choice follows the coolant, weight and cost limits.

Can you source UQD fittings separately?

Yes. Quick disconnects, manifolds and hoses can be consolidated with the cold plates or quoted as a standalone multi-SKU thermal order.
