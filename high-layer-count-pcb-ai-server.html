Engineering & sourcing notes

工程与采购要点

Above roughly 20 layers the board becomes a lamination programme. Each additional press cycle adds registration error, and the accumulated drift is what limits yield — not the ability to image fine traces.

Process & technical notes

  • Sequential lamination splits a high-layer board into sub-composites; each sub-composite is pressed and X-ray checked for registration before the final press, otherwise drift compounds invisibly.
  • Back-drill depth is specified by the remaining stub length, and the drill depth tolerance must account for board thickness variation across the panel.
  • Impedance is a function of the actual pressed dielectric thickness, so the stack-up used for quoting must be the one measured on the press coupon, not the nominal prepreg datasheet.

Buyer checklist

  • Provide a full stack-up with dielectric thicknesses, copper weights and the target impedance per net class, plus the allowed stub length.
  • Ask which layer counts the fabricator runs in steady volume and request the yield and first-pass data for that specific count.
  • Agree the coupon design and the report format per lot before the pilot, since re-qualifying a backplane is far more expensive than specifying it up front.

QC & inspection focus

  • Cross-section the press coupon for dielectric thickness and registration, and compare TDR results across panel positions rather than a single site.
  • Run thermal stress and ionic contamination checks on high-layer builds, since multiple press cycles increase the risk of latent delamination.

Lamination registration, back-drill stubs and impedance spread

压合对位、背钻残桩与阻抗离散

These notes are compiled per category from public engineering standards and the failure modes buyers most often meet. Figures are typical industry ranges; the binding version is the one confirmed against your drawing, target market and RFQ.

  • Each sequential lamination cycle adds a registration error budget; a 30-layer board built as four sub-composites accumulates more drift than one built as two, regardless of artwork quality.
  • Back-drill stub length is a per-net-class requirement: a stub acceptable on a low-rate control net can be a resonant discontinuity on a 224G link on the same board.
  • Impedance is set by the pressed dielectric thickness, which differs from the nominal prepreg datasheet; quoting from the nominal value is the most common source of a systematic offset.
  • Panel-level thickness variation changes the optimum back-drill depth across the same panel, so a single drill-depth setting cannot satisfy the tightest stub limit everywhere.

A real trade-off at RFQ stage

A buyer asks for a 30-layer board at a 10% impedance tolerance and a 0.2 mm maximum stub. The fabricator can hold either target alone, but the drill-depth spread across the panel makes the two conflict at the corners. We normally split the net classes in the RFQ — a tight stub limit only on the high-speed links — and require TDR coupons at multiple panel positions, so the trade-off is visible before the first press.

FAQ

常见问题

What is the minimum order quantity (MOQ)?

500 pcs per item. Mixed-SKU consolidation across one shipment is allowed.

How fast is the quote?

How can I verify the factory remotely?

What are the payment terms?

T/T to Shenzhen Senya Industrial Co., Ltd.: 30% deposit to start, 70% before shipment (ship after full payment).

Do you handle finished or powered devices?

Yes. We integrate assembled electronics and PCBA, and consolidate finished devices into one shipment with a full export document package.

What is a realistic layer count for a first build?

It depends on the fabricator's proven volume capability. Public 2026 reporting describes 20–30 layers as mainstream for AI computing boards; ultra-high-layer backplanes are a separate, tighter qualification.

Do you guarantee impedance across the whole panel?

Impedance tolerance is a defined, measured requirement with TDR coupons per panel. The target and the coupon location are agreed at RFQ and reported per lot.

Why does back-drill depth matter?

A leftover via stub acts as a resonant discontinuity at high data rates. Stub length is specified per net class rather than as one board-wide value.

Are the 40+ layer figures a capability you offer?

No. Those are publicly reported industry figures used as buyer context. Feasible layer count, yield and lead time are confirmed against your stack-up by the nominated fabricator at RFQ.

Is a higher layer count always needed for AI boards?

No. Layer count follows routing density, power distribution and signal-integrity isolation. Some designs trade layers for a more expensive low-loss material instead.

Can you quote a prototype below 500 pcs?

Yes. Prototype and pilot builds can start from a handful of boards; the MOQ applies to production runs and is stated in the written RFQ.
